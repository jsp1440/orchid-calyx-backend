# The Hidden Pattern Engine: Traits, Pollinators, and Genomes

The Orchid Continuum treats traits not as isolated labels but as evidence-bearing biological assertions that can be connected to ecological interactions and molecular data. A floral spur measurement, volatile compound, color state, phenology observation, pollinator visit, gene association, expression change, protein record, and sequence accession can all become parts of one inspectable evidence graph.

The scientific opportunity is to ask whether the same combinations recur. Do long-tongued pollinators repeatedly select for elongated floral structures? Do unrelated orchid lineages that converge on the same pollination syndrome use homologous developmental pathways, or do they reach the same phenotype through different molecular routes? Do particular scent chemistries recur with particular pollinator guilds? Can repeated trait–pollinator associations be connected to loci, regulatory programs, or expression signatures strongly enough to justify a new experiment?

Calyx therefore includes a Trait–Interaction–Genomics Discovery Engine. Its first responsibility is not to declare mechanisms. It searches for repeated patterns across taxa while preserving every supporting evidence record. Candidate discoveries remain explicitly non-causal until phylogenetic correction, replication, mechanistic evidence, and scientific review justify stronger language.

This design allows the system to grow from classical TraitBank-style data into a research platform. Traits may be observed directly, inherited from a taxonomic assertion, predicted computationally, or associated with genetic and selection evidence; those categories are never collapsed. Ecological relationships are normalized against shared vocabularies such as GloBI while genuinely novel literature-supported relationships are retained rather than rejected merely because an aggregator has not recorded them yet.

Molecular evidence adds another axis. Gene identifiers, protein identifiers, sequence accessions, transcript or expression records, and pathway references can be attached to the same taxon and trait context. Where expression matrices are available, validated biclustering methods such as QUBIC may later be used as dedicated analysis adapters. Their outputs must return to Calyx with software version, parameters, source matrix, and provenance so a pattern can be reproduced rather than merely asserted.

The operational memory for this work lives in Neon/Postgres because Calyx must query and revise evidence continuously. Approved scientific snapshots are packaged with checksums for publication to Zenodo, creating a citable, versioned public archive. Zenodo is therefore the preserved scientific record; Neon is the living research memory; temporary Render storage is only staging.

The live discovery path now reads the Orchid Continuum's canonical scientific stores directly. Resolved traits are preferred from `oc_views.trait_resolved_v4`; ecological interactions are drawn from the canonical Orchid Continuum interaction layer, including GloBI-normalized records where appropriate; and true molecular association tables are used when they exist. Existing phylogenetic sequence data are preserved as context, but sequence presence by itself is never promoted to evidence that a gene or marker controls a trait. That distinction matters: a sequence used to build a phylogeny is not the same thing as a demonstrated genotype–phenotype association.

The live adapter also refuses to join domains using a scientific name alone. It requires a recognized canonical taxon identifier so a synonym or homonym cannot quietly create a false pattern. Rows that cannot be linked rigorously are counted and retained as a data-integration problem rather than being guessed into the discovery engine. Likewise, missing source confidence is treated conservatively rather than as certainty.

With these boundaries, the archive loop becomes a reproducible research instrument: live trait evidence, ecological relationships, and molecular associations are assembled into a deterministic evidence snapshot; Calyx searches for repeated cross-taxon patterns; Neon preserves the working evidence and hypotheses; and a checksummed Zenodo draft can preserve an owner-reviewed version of the dataset without automatic public publication. Re-running the same evidence produces the same dataset identity and is prevented from creating duplicate archive deposits.

The long-term goal is a system capable of surfacing hidden evolutionary structure across Orchidaceae: repeated pollinator-mediated selection, convergent trait syndromes, candidate genetic architectures, and inheritance patterns that may not be visible within any single paper or database. Each proposed pattern is a hypothesis with a trail of evidence, not an answer without one.
