# Orchid Continuum Design Principles

## Principle 1 — Begin with a living orchid

Every journey should begin with something alive, beautiful, specific, and meaningful. The visitor should encounter an orchid before they encounter a database.

## Principle 2 — Reveal relationships

Every feature should help someone see a connection they could not see before.

## Principle 3 — Move from beauty to understanding

The ideal experience moves through wonder, curiosity, discovery, evidence, understanding, and stewardship.

## Principle 4 — Preserve provenance

Claims should be traceable to sources. The system should distinguish established knowledge, observation, inference, hypothesis, uncertainty, and imagination.

## Principle 5 — Adapt to the learner

The same scientific knowledge may be presented through story, image, map, graph, lesson, quiz, field activity, research summary, mind map, or conservation scenario.

## Principle 6 — Avoid dead ends

Every page, card, and visualization should answer: what am I looking at, why does it matter, how is it connected, and where do I go next?

## Principle 7 — Hide incompleteness gracefully

Do not present broken experiences as if they are complete. If images, relationships, or evidence are missing, explain the gap or hide the card until it can serve the visitor.

## Principle 8 — Treat community as knowledge-bearing

Community contributions should be welcomed, contextualized, and given provenance. Not all contributions are scientific evidence, but many can enrich understanding.

## Principle 9 — Make the graph alive

The Knowledge Graph should not merely display nodes. It should help users explore, question, compare, and discover relationships.

## Principle 10 — Let stewardship close the loop

Every experience should eventually point toward care: learning more, observing, conserving, growing responsibly, teaching, contributing, supporting, or protecting.
